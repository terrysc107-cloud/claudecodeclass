Micro-SaaS is the highest-leverage income model for a solo developer with Cursor. Small products solving specific problems for niche audiences. Recurring revenue. No VC funding required.

## The Micro-SaaS Formula

**Small problem + specific audience + recurring charge = business**

The key word is *small*. You're not building Salesforce. You're building the thing that saves a specific kind of business owner 2 hours a week and they'll happily pay $29/month forever for.

Good micro-SaaS ideas are hiding in:
- Manual spreadsheet workflows ("I do this in Excel every week")
- Recurring tasks that are always the same
- Reports that people wish updated automatically
- Data that lives in one system that should be in another

<Callout type="tip">
Before building anything, find 3 people who have the problem you're solving and ask them to pre-pay. Not "would you pay for this" — actually ask for money before the product exists. If they say yes, you have product-market fit. If they say "I'd probably use it" — that's a no. Build for the pre-payers.
</Callout>

## Niche Selection

Pick a niche where you have some existing knowledge or network. The product doesn't have to be interesting to anyone outside the niche — it just has to be indispensable to people in it.

High-signal niches to explore:
- Specific industries (real estate agents, gym owners, restaurant managers)
- Specific workflows (invoice automation, appointment scheduling, inventory tracking)
- Specific integrations (connecting two SaaS tools that don't talk to each other)

The narrower the niche, the less competition, and the more you can charge relative to your audience size.

## The Weekend Build

With Cursor, the MVP of a focused micro-SaaS can be built in a weekend:

**Saturday:**
- Scaffold the project (Next.js + Supabase + Stripe)
- Build the core feature (the one thing that solves the problem)
- Build auth and user management

**Sunday:**
- Build the billing flow (Stripe checkout + webhook)
- Build the marketing page with pricing
- Deploy to Vercel
- Set up the domain

<Callout type="warning">
Don't add features before getting your first paying customer. The MVP should do the core thing and nothing else. Every additional feature before validation is a bet that delays the moment you find out whether the core idea is worth pursuing. Ship lean, validate fast.
</Callout>

## Pricing for MRR

Recurring revenue math:
- $29/month × 50 customers = $1,450 MRR
- $49/month × 30 customers = $1,470 MRR
- $99/month × 15 customers = $1,485 MRR

For a niche tool solving a real pain point, 15–50 customers is achievable within 3–6 months through direct outreach and community participation in the target niche.

## Getting First Customers

Don't wait for SEO. Go find customers:

1. Join communities where your target customer spends time (Reddit, Slack groups, Discord, niche forums)
2. Answer questions related to the problem your tool solves
3. Mention the product naturally: "I actually built something for this — here's the link if you want to try it"
4. DM directly: "I saw you mentioned this problem in [community]. I built a tool that solves exactly that. Want to try it free for a month?"

The first 10 customers almost always come from direct outreach, not discovery.

<KeyPoint>A $29/month tool with 50 customers is $1,450/month. You don't need viral scale or a huge audience. You need 50 people with a specific problem who value a specific solution. Cursor makes the build fast enough that you can test multiple niches if the first one doesn't gain traction.</KeyPoint>
