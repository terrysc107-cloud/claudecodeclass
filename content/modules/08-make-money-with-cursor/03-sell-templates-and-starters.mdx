Templates are the highest-margin product a developer can sell. You build them once from work you were already doing, and they sell indefinitely with no additional time investment.

## What Sells

**SaaS starter templates** ($97–$197)
The full scaffold: auth, payments, database, deployment. A developer can clone it and have a working foundation in minutes instead of days. These sell because they save significant setup time on every project.

**Landing page templates** ($29–$97)
Clean, conversion-optimized Next.js or React landing page with multiple sections and easy customization. Every business needs a landing page. Developers who don't want to design from scratch will pay.

**Niche-specific starters** ($49–$197)
A starter configured for a specific use case: a course platform, a job board, a directory site, a client portal. More specific = higher price for the right buyer.

**Automation scripts** ($19–$49)
Python or Node scripts that solve a specific recurring problem: CSV processing, API data sync, scheduled reports. Low price, high volume potential.

**.cursorrules packs** ($9–$29)
Collections of well-crafted cursorrules files for specific stacks or frameworks. Surprisingly in demand among developers who use Cursor but haven't built good rules yet.

<Callout type="tip">
Bundle a short video walkthrough (screen recording, 10–15 min) with any template over $49. The video shows the product in action, demonstrates the quality, and helps buyers understand how to customize it. A $97 template with a video routinely outsells a $97 template without one.
</Callout>

## Preparing a Template for Sale

1. **Remove all client-specific data** — no client names, no real API keys, no hardcoded business logic
2. **Extract everything to environment variables** — all secrets, URLs, and configuration
3. **Add comprehensive `.cursorrules`** — this is a key differentiator for dev-focused templates
4. **Write a setup guide** — from git clone to deployed in the simplest possible steps
5. **Add screenshots or a demo** — buyers need to see what they're getting
6. **Write a clear description** — what does this solve, who is it for, what's included

## Platforms

**Gumroad** — easiest to set up, no monthly fee, 10% + payment processing fee. Best for starting out.

**LemonSqueezy** — more developer-friendly, lower fees at scale, better analytics.

**Your own site** — highest margins, requires marketing. Build to here after you have an audience.

## Marketing Templates

Without an audience, templates are hard to sell by sitting on a marketplace. They need to be discovered. Channels that work:

- **Twitter/X** — post a thread about how you built it, what problems it solves, link to purchase
- **Reddit** — post in relevant subreddits (r/nextjs, r/webdev, etc.) — be genuinely helpful, mention the template where relevant
- **YouTube** — a short video tutorial about the problem the template solves, with a link to the template in the description
- **Communities** — Discord servers, Slack groups, niche forums where your target buyer hangs out

<Callout type="info">
Content about the problem drives more sales than content about the product. "How to set up Stripe webhooks in Next.js" attracts people who then discover your SaaS starter template. Solve their problem for free in a blog post or video, then sell them the shortcut.
</Callout>

## The Catalog Math

5 templates × $100 average price × 10 sales/month each = $5,000/month passive income.

This is achievable within 12–18 months of consistently building and marketing. It doesn't happen overnight, but it compounds — each new template benefits from the audience built by previous ones.

<KeyPoint>You build it once, it sells while you sleep — templates are the highest-margin product a developer can sell. Every project you complete is a template waiting to be packaged. Start treating finished work as assets, not just deliverables.</KeyPoint>
