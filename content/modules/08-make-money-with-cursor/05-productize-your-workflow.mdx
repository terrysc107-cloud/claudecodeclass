The developer who sells a product earns more than the developer who sells hours. Productizing means packaging your process into a defined, repeatable offer with a fixed scope, fixed price, and fixed timeline. Cursor makes this possible because it makes your delivery predictable.

## What Productizing Means

A productized service has three fixed elements:

1. **Fixed scope** — you do exactly this, and nothing more
2. **Fixed price** — the same price for every client
3. **Fixed timeline** — you deliver in this many days/weeks, period

Example productized service:
> **"Landing Page Sprint" — $1,200 — delivered in 5 business days**
> - One full-page marketing landing page
> - Hero, features, testimonials, and pricing sections
> - Deployed to Vercel with your domain connected
> - Includes one round of revisions

A client either buys this or they don't. There's no negotiation, no custom quote, no open-ended scope. You either fit their needs or you don't.

<Callout type="tip">
The hardest part of productizing is drawing the scope boundary and holding it. "Can you also add a contact form?" is scope creep. "That's not included in the Landing Page Sprint, but I can add it as an add-on for $200" is how you handle it. The boundary is what makes the product work.
</Callout>

## Why Cursor Makes This Possible

Productized services only work when delivery is predictable. If a landing page sometimes takes 5 days and sometimes takes 3 weeks, you can't sell it at a fixed price.

Cursor makes delivery predictable:
- The scaffold is consistent (your starter template)
- The implementation is fast (Composer + Cmd+K)
- The debugging is faster (Chat-assisted)
- The documentation is generated (not written from scratch)

When you've delivered the same type of project 10 times, you have a reliable estimate. Turn that into a productized offer.

## Your First Productized Offer

Start with something you've already done at least 3–5 times. That experience gives you the confidence that you can deliver it reliably.

Write the offer page:
- What is included (specific, not vague)
- What is not included (equally specific)
- Price (fixed, visible, no "contact for a quote")
- Timeline (specific days, not "2–4 weeks")
- Who it's for (be specific about the ideal client)
- Who it's not for (be equally specific)

<Callout type="info">
A productized service page that says who it's NOT for builds more trust than one that tries to appeal to everyone. "This is not for enterprise companies or projects requiring custom backend integrations" filters out bad-fit clients and signals confidence to good-fit clients.
</Callout>

## The Audit Entry Point

Not every client is ready to buy a full project. Offer a productized audit first:

> **"Technical Audit" — $300 — delivered in 2 business days**
> I'll review your current site or app and deliver a prioritized report of:
> - Performance issues
> - UX problems affecting conversions
> - Technical debt risks
> - 3 highest-impact improvements with rough implementation estimates

An audit is low-commitment for the client, fast for you (with Cursor), and almost always converts to a larger project. It's also immediately profitable: $300 for 3–4 hours of work is a healthy rate.

## Scaling the Productized Model

Once one offer is running smoothly:

1. Add a second offer (a different scope/price tier)
2. Build a waitlist for when you're fully booked
3. Eventually hire a developer to deliver the lower-tier offer while you focus on the higher-margin work

This is how a solo developer builds an agency without losing control of quality.
