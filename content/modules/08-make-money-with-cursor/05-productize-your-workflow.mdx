The developer who sells a product earns more than the developer who sells hours. Productizing means packaging your process into a defined, repeatable offer with a fixed scope, fixed price, and fixed timeline. Claude Code makes this possible because it makes your delivery predictable enough to put a price and deadline on.

## What Productizing Means

A productized service has three fixed elements:

1. **Fixed scope** — you do exactly this, and nothing more
2. **Fixed price** — the same price for every client
3. **Fixed timeline** — you deliver in this many days/weeks, period

Example productized service:
> **"Landing Page Sprint" — $1,200 — delivered in 5 business days**
> - One full-page marketing landing page
> - Hero, features, testimonials, and pricing sections
> - Deployed to Vercel with your domain connected
> - Includes one round of revisions

A client either buys this or they don't. There's no negotiation, no custom quote, no open-ended scope. You either fit their needs or you don't.

<Callout type="tip">
The hardest part of productizing is drawing the scope boundary and holding it. "Can you also add a contact form?" is scope creep. "That's not included in the Landing Page Sprint, but I can add it as an add-on for $200" is how you handle it. The boundary is what makes the product work — and it's what makes pricing reliable.
</Callout>

## Why Claude Code Makes This Possible

Productized services only work when delivery is predictable. If a landing page sometimes takes 5 days and sometimes takes 3 weeks, you can't sell it at a fixed price.

Claude Code makes delivery predictable:
- The scaffold is consistent (your CLAUDE.md-powered starter template)
- Implementation of boilerplate is fast — auth, forms, components, API integrations
- Debugging is faster with Claude Code as a sounding board
- Documentation is generated, not written from scratch

When you've delivered the same type of project 10 times with Claude Code, you have a reliable, calibrated estimate. Turn that into a productized offer.

## Your First Productized Offer

Start with something you've already done at least 3–5 times. That experience gives you the confidence that you can deliver it reliably, and it tells you where the scope edges are.

Write the offer page:
- What is included (specific, not vague)
- What is not included (equally specific)
- Price (fixed, visible, no "contact for a quote")
- Timeline (specific days, not "2–4 weeks")
- Who it's for (be specific about the ideal client)
- Who it's not for (be equally specific)

<Callout type="info">
A productized service page that says who it's NOT for builds more trust than one that tries to appeal to everyone. "This is not for enterprise companies or projects requiring custom backend integrations" filters out bad-fit clients and signals confidence to good-fit clients. Specificity converts better than breadth.
</Callout>

## Building the Systems Around Your Offer

A productized service is only as scalable as the systems supporting it. For each offer you create:

**A starter template** — a GitHub template repo with your go-to stack, pre-configured, with a `CLAUDE.md` that describes the project type. Every time you take on this offer, you start from the template.

**A prompt library** — a document with the prompts you use for this offer type. The scaffold prompt, the component generation prompt, the review prompt. Don't rewrite them from scratch each time.

**A handoff template** — a document structure for the final delivery. What to include, how to format it, what the client needs to know.

With these systems, you can start a new project in the offer category and be productive within 15 minutes. The overhead of starting from scratch disappears.

## The Audit Entry Point

Not every client is ready to buy a full project. Offer a productized audit first:

> **"Technical Audit" — $300 — delivered in 2 business days**
> I'll review your current site or app and deliver a prioritized report:
> - Performance issues
> - UX problems affecting conversions
> - Technical debt risks
> - 3 highest-impact improvements with rough implementation estimates

An audit is low-commitment for the client, fast for you (with Claude Code, a thorough codebase review takes hours, not days), and almost always converts to a larger project. It's also immediately profitable.

## Stacking Your Offer Suite

Once one offer is running smoothly, add a second at a different price point:

| Offer | Price | Timeline | What it is |
|-------|-------|----------|------------|
| Technical Audit | $300 | 2 days | Review + report |
| Landing Page Sprint | $1,200 | 5 days | Full landing page |
| SaaS MVP | $4,500 | 3 weeks | Core product + billing |
| Internal Tool | $3,000–$8,000 | 2–4 weeks | Custom business tool |

Each offer serves a different client need and budget. The audit feeds into the Sprint, the Sprint feeds into the MVP, and so on. The client who buys the audit at $300 is the most likely buyer for the next tier.

## Scaling the Productized Model

Once offers are running smoothly:

1. Build a waitlist for when you're fully booked
2. Raise prices when the waitlist is consistently full
3. Eventually hire a developer to deliver the lower-tier offer while you focus on higher-margin work

This is how a solo developer builds an agency without losing control of quality. Claude Code gives the economics to get there: your output per hour is high enough that even after bringing on another developer, the margins remain strong.
