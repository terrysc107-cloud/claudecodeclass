Internal tools are one of the highest-paying and lowest-competition niches in development. A custom dashboard that gives a business owner real-time visibility into their operations is worth thousands of dollars — and there's no off-the-shelf product that does exactly what they need.

## Why Internal Tools Pay Well

Internal tools are:
- **Essential** — the business genuinely can't operate without them after they have them
- **Invisible to competitors** — nobody knows what's running behind the scenes
- **Custom by definition** — no generic SaaS can solve a company-specific workflow

A business paying $5,000 for a custom reporting dashboard that saves their team 10 hours/week has an ROI that's obvious: the tool pays for itself in weeks at typical labor costs. The price feels like nothing compared to the value.

## What Businesses Actually Need

**Reporting dashboards** — real-time visibility into sales, operations, inventory, or customer data. Most businesses run on spreadsheets or drill into multiple systems. A single dashboard pulling it all together is immediately valuable.

**Operations tools** — internal apps for managing orders, assignments, bookings, or staff schedules. Often replacing something built in Excel or a cobbled-together spreadsheet system.

**CRM integrations** — connecting sales tools to marketing tools to billing systems. Data that currently requires manual export/import, now flowing automatically.

**Client portals** — branded portals where clients can view project status, approve work, download deliverables, or submit requests. High perceived value for the client's clients.

**Data processing scripts** — tools that transform raw data exports into useful reports, summaries, or alerts. Often Python scripts that run on a schedule.

<Callout type="tip">
When scoping an internal tool project, ask: "What does someone do manually today that this tool will automate?" Then price the tool based on how much of that manual time it eliminates. A tool that eliminates 4 hours per week at $30/hour labor cost saves $6,240 per year. Charging $3,000–$5,000 for it is an obvious yes for any business owner who understands the math.
</Callout>

## Setting Up for Internal Tool Projects

Create a `CLAUDE.md` template specifically for internal tool engagements:

```markdown
# Project: [Client Name] — [Tool Name]
# Stack: Next.js, Supabase, [any third-party APIs]

## What This Does
[One paragraph: what the tool does, who uses it, what problem it solves]

## Business Process Being Automated
[Step-by-step description of the manual process this replaces]

## Key Entities
[The main data objects: e.g., Customer, Order, Invoice, Staff Member]

## Integrations
[Any external systems this connects to and what data flows between them]

## Users
[Who uses this tool and what level of access they have]

## Constraints
[Anything the tool must not do, performance requirements, data sensitivity]
```

This `CLAUDE.md` keeps Claude oriented to the business context, not just the code, which produces much more relevant suggestions on domain-specific logic.

## The Retainer Model

Internal tools don't stay static. The business grows, requirements change, integrations break. After delivering an internal tool, offer a maintenance retainer:

- $300–$800/month for ongoing support, bug fixes, and small improvements
- Quarterly scope sessions for larger feature requests (billed separately)
- Priority response SLA — if something breaks, you fix it within 24 hours

A business that depends on an internal tool will pay for this without question. And 3–5 retainers at $500/month is $1,500–$2,500/month in stable recurring revenue from existing clients.

Talk to small and mid-size business owners — not startups. Ask: "What do you do manually that you wish happened automatically?" or "If you could press a button and have one report appear, what would it show?"

Their answers are your project pipeline. Every business owner has at least one answer to these questions. Most have three or four. The constraint has always been the cost and time of custom development — Claude Code changes that equation, and you're the one who benefits.
