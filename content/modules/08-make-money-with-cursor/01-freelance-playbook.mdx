Claude Code gives you a fundamental advantage over freelancers who don't use it: you can deliver better work in less time. The question is how to use that advantage — in pricing, positioning, and client selection.

## Finding Clients

The three highest-ROI channels for developers:

**Referrals** — the best clients come from existing clients. Deliver exceptional work, then ask: "Do you know anyone else who could use what we built together?" One great client referral network beats any platform.

**LinkedIn** — post content about what you build. Not vanity posts — show the work. A screenshot of a feature you shipped, a short description of a problem you solved. Developers who build in public attract inbound clients without cold outreach.

**Direct outreach** — find businesses with obvious technical problems (slow site, broken checkout, no landing page) and message the owner directly. Keep it specific: "I noticed your checkout page isn't mobile-optimized — I can fix that for you this week."

<Callout type="tip">
When you're starting out, do one great project at a reduced rate for a business you admire. Build something that genuinely moves their business. Then use it as a case study (with their permission). One documented case study — problem, solution, result — is worth 20 portfolio pieces.
</Callout>

## Positioning Claude Code as a Feature

Don't hide that you use AI-assisted development tools. Lead with it:

"I use Claude Code, an AI development tool from Anthropic, that lets me ship working products in days instead of weeks. This means you pay for less calendar time, get to market faster, and I spend more of my focus on the design decisions that actually matter — not boilerplate implementation."

This is honest, compelling, and differentiating. Most clients will see it as a positive, not a concern. The clients who push back aren't your clients anyway — they're optimizing for something other than results.

## Setting Up a Client Project with Claude Code

When you start a client engagement, set up the project to work efficiently with Claude Code from day one:

1. Create the repo and scaffold the project
2. Write a `CLAUDE.md` that captures the client's requirements, constraints, and conventions
3. Include domain context: what the client's business does, their users, their technical constraints
4. Update `CLAUDE.md` as you learn more about the project

A well-maintained `CLAUDE.md` means every session you start on the project gets productive immediately — no re-explaining context, no starting from scratch. This is a direct multiplier on your delivery speed.

## Pricing Your Work

Two frameworks:

**Value-based pricing**: What is this worth to the client? A checkout that converts 2% better on $100k/month revenue is worth $2,000/month. What fraction of that value is reasonable to charge as a one-time build?

**Day-rate pricing**: What would a reasonable day's work be worth? Many experienced freelance developers charge $800–$2,000/day. With Claude Code, your effective output per day is higher, which justifies the upper end of that range.

Start with the value-based estimate. Use the day-rate as a floor. Charge whichever is higher.

<Callout type="info">
"I can't afford that" usually means "I don't see the value yet." Your job isn't to lower the price — it's to articulate the value more clearly. A $2,000 landing page that generates $5,000 in new business in the first month isn't expensive. Walk through that math with the client.
</Callout>

## Delivery That Earns Referrals

The things that make clients refer you:

1. **Ship what you said you would, when you said you would** — most freelancers don't, which means doing this alone puts you in the top tier
2. **Communicate before they have to ask** — a brief update every 2 days is enough
3. **Deliver documentation** — a short handoff doc showing what was built and how to maintain it signals professionalism
4. **Propose the next thing** — at delivery, mention one improvement you noticed. "I also saw an opportunity to optimize the email notifications — worth a conversation when you're ready."

Claude Code makes all of these easier. You deliver faster, so communication is easier. You can generate documentation rather than write it from scratch. You can scope additional work confidently because you can estimate based on real delivery speed.

## After the First Client

Raise your rate for the next one. Every project you complete is experience and credibility. Price accordingly.

The gap between your delivery speed and a client's expectations is your competitive advantage. Most freelancers take weeks to deliver what you can deliver in days. That difference is your moat — price into it, don't discount it away.

<KeyPoint>Claude Code is your delivery infrastructure, not just a coding tool. Lead with it as a feature in client conversations, set up every engagement with a good CLAUDE.md, and charge rates that reflect your actual output — not the industry average for developers who work without it.</KeyPoint>
